## Part 1 — When Formal Probate May Not Be Needed

California law provides several ways to transfer a deceased person's (decedent's) property without a
full, formal probate case.

### Government benefits

Most government benefits, like Social Security survivor benefits, can be collected without going to
probate court.

### Property with named beneficiaries

Common property with beneficiary designations includes:

- Life insurance proceeds
- Bank or retirement accounts with a designated beneficiary
- Pensions
- Annuities
- Property held in a living trust

Surviving named beneficiaries can transfer this property to themselves without probate court
involvement.

### Property with survivorship rights (joint ownership)

- **Joint tenancy:** the surviving owner automatically receives the property.
- **Transfer on death (TOD):** the property transfers automatically to the listed owner.
- **Community property:** community property between spouses/registered domestic partners may
  include survivorship rights.
- **Joint bank accounts:** can include survivorship rights, in which case they pass to the
  surviving owner outside probate.

### Small estates: the primary simplified method

Estates whose qualifying value is at or below a set threshold can use simplified ("summary
succession") transfer procedures instead of full probate.

**Estate value limits (Probate Code §13050 valuation; §§13100–13101 procedure):**

| Date of death | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $166,250 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $184,500 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | $208,850 |

**40-day waiting period:** If the total value of these assets is at the set amount or less **and 40
days have passed since the death**, you can transfer personal property using an Affidavit (a small
estate affidavit, Probate Code §§13100–13101).

#### Property INCLUDED when valuing the estate

- All real and personal property of the decedent
- Life insurance or retirement benefits payable **to the estate** (rather than to designated
  beneficiaries)

#### Property EXCLUDED when valuing the estate

- Vehicles — cars, boats, and mobile homes
- Real property located outside California
- Property held in trust (including a living trust)
- Real or personal property owned in joint tenancy (passes to the surviving joint owner)
- Community, quasi-community, or separate property that passes to the surviving spouse or
  registered domestic partner
- Life insurance, death benefits, and other assets not subject to probate that pass directly to
  named beneficiaries
- Unpaid salary/compensation up to the limits stated in Form DE-300 (Probate Code §13050(c))
- Bank accounts with multiple owners (survivorship accounts)
- **Debts and mortgages may NOT be subtracted** — the thresholds compare against gross qualifying
  value, not net value.

### Community property: spousal / domestic partner procedure

A surviving spouse or registered domestic partner who is legally entitled to the decedent's
property can file a **Spousal or Domestic Partner Property Petition (Form DE-221)** — much faster
than a full probate case.

Note: the spouses may also have had an agreement (a prenuptial agreement, or "prenup") providing
that an asset is not community property.

**Required documentation:** deeds, bank statements, marriage or domestic partnership records; and
review the will (if any) to confirm the property was not left to someone else.

**The court order confirms:**

- The surviving spouse/partner's own one-half share of the community property, and
- The surviving spouse/partner's entitlement to the decedent's share of community property and any
  separate property passing to them.

## Part 2 — Small Estate Affidavit to Transfer Personal Property (§§13100–13106)

This process allows a person with legal inheritance rights (an entitled successor, under the will
or intestate succession) to collect or transfer the decedent's **personal property** without a
formal probate case.

### Eligibility requirements — ALL must be met

1. The estate's qualifying value does not exceed the small estate threshold for the date of death
   ($166,250 for deaths before April 1, 2022; $184,500 for deaths April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025;
   $208,850 for deaths on or after April 1, 2025 — check current limits on Form DE-300).
2. The property being transferred with the affidavit is **personal property only** (not real
   property like houses, buildings, or land).
3. **No formal probate case has been opened** for the estate.
4. **At least 40 days have passed since the death.**

### Prohibited uses

- The affidavit cannot transfer real property (houses, buildings, land). For real property of
  small value, see the **Affidavit re Real Property of Small Value (Form DE-305)** instead.
- The affidavit cannot be used if a probate case has already been opened — in that situation you
  need the written permission of the estate's personal representative.

### Required documents

1. A certified copy of the death certificate
2. Proof the decedent owned the property (bank statements, passbook, stock certificate, storage
   receipt)
3. Proof of your own identity (driver's license, passport)
4. An Inventory and Appraisal (Form DE-160) of any real property the decedent owned, if applicable

### Step-by-step process

1. **Gather documentation** — certified death certificate, ownership proof, identity documents.
2. **Obtain the affidavit form** — many banks and companies have their own form; otherwise use a
   court Self-Help Center, law library, or a sample form.
3. **Complete the affidavit** — all property can be listed in one affidavit, or separate
   affidavits can be used per item.
4. **Attach supporting documents** — death certificate, ownership documentation, identity proof,
   and DE-160 if applicable.
5. **Notarization** — not legally required, but most institutions request a notarized affidavit.
6. **Obtain signatures** — if other people are also entitled to inherit the property, they must
   also sign the affidavit, showing everyone agrees to the transfer.
7. **Present the affidavit** to the bank/company/entity holding the property.

### If an institution refuses the affidavit

Some institutions may wrongly insist on "Letters" (formal probate appointment documents). Refer
them to **California Probate Code sections 13100 to 13106**, or escalate to the institution's
management or legal department.

## Part 3 — Maximum Property Values for Summary Succession Procedures (Prob. Code §890)

California law lets you use simpler "summary succession" procedures if the property is worth less
than a set amount. The dollar limits are adjusted every 3 years under Probate Code §890 (last
updates: April 1, 2022 and April 1, 2025; next update: April 1, 2028). Current values are
published on **Form DE-300** (Maximum Values for Small Estate Set-Aside & Disposition of Estate
Without Administration).

### Probate Code §§6602, 6609 — small estate set-aside

**Purpose:** a limited amount can be set aside for the immediate needs of the surviving spouse and
minor children.

| Date of death | Limit |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $85,900 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $95,325 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | $107,900 |

### Probate Code §13050(c) — excluded compensation

**Purpose:** excludes certain property (unpaid military/employment compensation) from the estate
value calculation.

| Date of death | Limit |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $16,625 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $18,450 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | $20,875 |

### Probate Code §§13100, 13101 — small estate affidavit (personal property)

**Purpose:** collect or transfer personal property without full probate.

| Date of death | Limit |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $166,250 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $184,500 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | $208,850 |

### Probate Code §§13151–13154 — petition to determine succession to real property (Form DE-310)

**Purpose:** transfer the decedent's home without full probate.

| Date of death | Limit |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $166,250 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $184,500 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | **$750,000** |

**2025 change (Assembly Bill 2016, Prob. Code §§13150–13157):** the limit was raised to $750,000,
but the procedure was restricted to the decedent's **primary residence (main home) located in
California** only.

### Probate Code §13200 — affidavit re real property of small value (Form DE-305)

**Purpose:** request transfer of real property of small value.

| Date of death | Limit |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $55,425 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $61,500 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | $69,625 |

### Probate Code §§13600–13601 — surviving spouse collects earnings

**Purpose:** a surviving spouse collects the deceased spouse's unpaid earnings from an employer.

| Date of death | Limit |
|---|---|
| Before April 1, 2022 | $16,625 |
| April 1, 2022 – March 31, 2025 | $18,450 |
| On or after April 1, 2025 | $20,875 |

## Key takeaways

- Full probate can often be avoided when the qualifying property value is under the applicable
  threshold.
- Value limits depend on the **date of death** and the **type of property**.
- Limits are updated every 3 years (Prob. Code §890); next adjustment April 1, 2028.
- A 40-day waiting period after the death applies before the small estate affidavit can be used.
- The 2025 law (AB 2016) raised the real property petition limit to $750,000, for the decedent's
  main California home only.
- Debts and mortgages cannot be subtracted when valuing the estate against the thresholds.
